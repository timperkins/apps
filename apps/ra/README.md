# RA

A pixel-art real-time strategy graphics test at [ra.trom.dev](https://ra.trom.dev).

- **Picking a map**: when the game loads (and on **New map** in the menu) you pick one of four maps, each shown as a little picture with your start and the enemy base marked; **Shuffle** shows other versions of them. The maps are in `src/v2/maps.ts`:
  - **Long Isle** (the first, for testing): one long island in a world that is three quarters ocean, you at one end and the enemy at the other, so there is really only one way to go.
  - **Equator**: a belt of land round the middle of the planet, half ocean, with two ways round to the enemy.
  - **Lakelands**: grassland all the way round with lakes and ridges.
  - **Highlands**: dry and rugged, few lakes and plateaus everywhere.
- **Stage** (for testing): buttons above the maps pick how the game starts, and the last pick is remembered.
  - **Initial**: the normal start, below.
  - **Combat**: straight into a fight. You get a full base of your own (Foundry, everything on the tech tree, generators to run it and a hauler at work), an army the size of the enemy's and $50000 (you can hold $100000), about a screen and a half from the enemy base. The enemy keeps its army up to strength: whenever it is short of a kind, its Barracks, Vehicle works or Helipad makes another, for free, until that building is destroyed. Its units wait in groups of four at six posts round its base, with new ones gathering by its Foundry until there are enough to fill a gap. They fight whatever comes near but never go looking for a fight. The enemy's side is in the "The enemy (Combat stage)" part of `src/v2/game.ts`.
- The world is a small planet, about fourteen screens across (10240 × 5120 map pixels, half the area it used to be): a procedurally generated landscape of grass, rocky ridges, forests, lakes and oceans with beaches and shimmering surf, and a dirt road from your start to the enemy base. Each map shapes the land with a mask added to the lake noise and its own share of water and high ground (`LandShape` in `src/v2/terrain.ts`). It is far too big to paint at once, so the ground is classified and painted in 128-pixel tiles as they come into view, a few each frame (`src/tiles.ts`), with a coarse overview of the whole map filling in until they are ready.
- The world wraps round in every direction: pan any way and you come back round, with the land flowing on seamlessly and everything the same way up (terrain noise is periodic both ways: `wrapNoise` and `WorldField` in `src/gfx.ts`). Units go the way you panned, so you can attack from either side.
- Zoom out past half size and you whoosh up through the atmosphere into space, where the world is a hazy, cloudy globe (`src/planet.ts`) with the map wrapped round it from the point under the camera, so panning slides the map round the globe without stretching it. The globe starts out bigger than the screen and keeps shrinking as you zoom out. It shows the overview of the map: lakes, roads, forests as darker land, the bases themselves, and units as dots of their colour. Drag it to spin it. Tap it to fly down, or, with units selected, to send them there the shortest way.
- At normal zoom a minimap globe sits at the top right with the view outlined. Drag it to spin the world quickly, or tap it to jump there.
- You play Cobalt, and you start with a handful of units and no base: a **Mobile Foundry** (a big tracked vehicle with a crane folded on top), two Lancer tanks, a Scout buggy and three Riflemen (in v2; v1 still starts with ready-made bases). The enemy, Ember, is dug in on the far side of the planet with a full base (Foundry, HQ, vehicle works, generators, barracks, refinery, silo, radar, helipad, gun towers) and an army: tanks, scout buggies, an ore hauler, artillery, a copter, infantry and patrol boats. The enemy has no plan of its own yet, but it fights back (see **Combat**). You only command your own side: tap an enemy unit or building to see its health, outlined in red.
- **Water**: ground units can't drive into water (lakes or the sea) and boats can't leave it. Heading into the shore they slide along it towards where they were sent, and give up when that gets them nowhere; there is no proper pathfinding yet.
- **Deploying the Foundry**: select the Mobile Foundry and its footprint shows on the ground, green where it can deploy (open, level grass clear of roads, water and other buildings) and red where it can't. Tap **Deploy** above the toolbar (or tap the vehicle again, or press F) and it lines up, anyone standing in the way drives off, and the Foundry rises out of the ground, clearing the trees. Tap the Foundry to select it and **Pack up** folds it back into the vehicle, so you can move your base.
- **Building your base**: once the Foundry stands, **Build** appears on the toolbar and your credits ($5000 to start, shown at the top) appear next to the menu button. Build opens the build menu: a sheet above the toolbar on a phone, a sidebar under the minimap on a wide screen (B toggles it). It lists only what you can build right now, in tabs (Base, Defense). Tap an item to start it: its icon darkens and a clock wipe sweeps round as the credits drain. Tap it again to put it on hold, and hold it (or right-click) to cancel and get your credits back. Each tab builds one thing at a time. When a building is ready, tap it and a see-through ghost of it goes on the map, green where it can stand (open, level ground within a short gap of the rest of your base) and red where it can't. On a phone, tap or drag to move it and press **Place**; with a mouse it follows the pointer and a click puts it down (right-click or Esc to put it back for later). It rises out of the ground.
- **The tech tree**: each building opens up more. Foundry → Generator → Barracks and Ore refinery; Barracks → Gun tower; Refinery → Ore silo, Vehicle works and Radar mast; Radar → Helipad; Vehicle works + Radar → Tech lab. New options get a **New** badge and a message. The tree is in `src/v2/tech.ts`.
- **Training units**: the Barracks opens the **Infantry** tab (Riflemen, Rocketeers) and the Vehicle works the **Vehicles** tab (Scout buggy and Lancer tank; Ore hauler with a refinery; Howler artillery with a radar; Bastion tank with a Tech lab; another Mobile Foundry), and a Helipad plus a Tech lab add the Kestrel copter. Units queue up: tap an item once per unit (up to 5 of each, the count shows on the icon) and hold it to take one off. Each tab makes one unit at a time; it drives out of the door, or lifts off the helipad. Select a Barracks, Vehicle works or Helipad and tap the ground to set its **rally point**, shown as a flag, where its new units go (**Clear rally** removes it). With two of the same kind, **Make primary** picks which one they come out of.
- **Ore and credits**: fields of golden ore grow round the planet, two of them near where you start, one by the enemy base and one half way: low heaps of rough nuggets on ore-stained earth, packed close so a field reads as a lumpy carpet. Every Ore refinery comes with a free Ore hauler, which finds the nearest ore, digs a load, drives it back to the refinery's striped pad and unloads it for credits, over and over. Clumps shrink as they are dug and creep back slowly unless dug out completely. Send a hauler somewhere else and it stops work; send it onto ore (or tap **Harvest**) and it starts again. You can only hold so many credits ($3000, plus $2000 per refinery and $3000 per Ore silo); when you are full, haulers wait at the refinery until you spend some or build a silo.
- **Combat** (`src/v2/combat.ts`): every weapon has a warhead and every unit and building an armour type, and a table says how much gets through: small arms shred infantry and barely scratch tanks, armour-piercing rounds are the other way round, and high explosive is good against buildings. Each weapon has its own damage, reload and range (in cells, about a tank's length), and only rockets and the Bastion's twin 105mm can hit the copter. Units standing about fire on the nearest enemy in range (gun towers too), a unit that is hit turns on whoever hit it and its friends nearby come to help (chasing only so far before going back), and a unit sent somewhere doesn't stop to fight. With units selected, tap an enemy (or right-click it) to attack it: the pointer turns into a red gunsight and they close in until it is in range. Direct-fire rounds land all over the target, not just its middle (`HIT_SPREAD` in `src/v2/combat.ts`). Rounds fly as tracers, shells, rockets with smoke trails or lobbed artillery shells that burst over an area; guns flash and kick back, and whatever is hit flashes and shows its health for a moment. Soldiers are knocked back a little by every hit, and tanks by heavy rounds (the Bastion's shells and artillery bursts), away from where the round came from (`knockback` in `src/v2/combat.ts`).
- **Destruction**: a damaged vehicle smokes from two thirds health, wisps at first, thickening to a dark column as it nears the end (`Fx.damageSmoke`). Anything destroyed blows up and breaks into small solid black pieces (its own shape cut up, `Fx.wreck` and `Fx.shatter` in `src/v2/combat.ts`), which land round it and fade away in about a second, while thick smoke rolls up and a column of it keeps rising from the spot for a few seconds; a copter's chunks rain down from the sky. Tanks cook off in a string of explosions with a fountain of embers. A building below half health is holed, scorched and on fire; when it is destroyed it goes up in a string of explosions, breaks into chunks under a cloud of smoke and leaves a ruin, which smokes for half a minute or so and stays for good. When a tank or a building is destroyed the view shakes a little, most when it is in the middle of the screen, not at all when it is off screen, and less the further out you zoom (`shake()` in `src/v2/game.ts`, applied in `src/main.ts`). The damaged buildings and ruins are drawn from the same models as the buildings (`makeBuildingStates` in `src/v2/sprites.ts`).
- **Power**: Generators make power and most other buildings use it. The build menu shows power used / made; when you use more than you make, the credits at the top turn red with a ⚡ and everything builds at half speed until you build another Generator. The numbers are in `src/v2/tech.ts`.
- **Moving round and selecting**: on a phone, drag to pan. Tap a unit to select it, or tap a building to select it and see its health. On a computer, drag a box to select several (WASD or the arrow keys pan).
  - **Select** on the toolbar (Q) opens the selection menu above it; selecting something opens it too. It has a button for each kind of unit of yours on screen, with a bubble saying how many there are: tap one to add them all to the selection (it lights up green), tap it again to take them out. You can have several kinds selected at once. **All** (shown while anything on screen isn't selected) selects everything of yours on screen, and **Clear** deselects.
  - With units selected, tap the ground to send them there or an enemy to attack it. On a phone a round green check comes up where you tapped, with the move cursor's green arrowheads turning round it (for an attack, a red one inside the attack cursor's gunsight): tap the check to send them, or somewhere else to move it. Clear the selection to call it off. With a mouse a click sends them straight away. Units on their way somewhere you sent them show a faint green line out to where they are going. Once you have sent them, the selection is locked as a group: the menu shows just those units (wherever they go), in a dashed frame, and taps and clicks send them again, even onto your own units. Clear (or Esc) to pick something else.
  - **Home** takes you back to your base (the Foundry, or the Foundry vehicle before it deploys).
- Pinch to zoom on a phone (two fingers also pan); on a computer, scroll to zoom around the cursor, or use + / - / 0.
- On a computer: shift adds to the selection, right-click moves (or attacks), middle-drag, WASD or the arrow keys pan, Q opens the selection menu, Ctrl+A selects everything on screen, F deploys the Foundry, H goes home, B opens the build menu, Esc clears.
- The menu button at the top left holds **New map**, which picks a map for a fresh world, and the version picker, which switches versions (also `?v=1` / `?v=2` in the URL; the last pick is remembered):
  - **v2 · Isometric** (default): 2:1 isometric view with raised plateaus and rock faces, and every unit, building, tree and prop drawn as a shaded voxel shape with cast shadows (`src/v2/`).
  - **v1 · Top-down**: the original straight-down oblique view (`src/v1/`).

No collision yet, and cliffs don't block units yet. The showcase at `/units/` shows every unit and building attacking, being hit, being destroyed (flying to pieces, and a building's ruin), and damaged. All art is drawn in code (`sprites.ts` and `terrain.ts` in each version's folder) and scaled up with nearest-neighbour filtering.

**How v2's art is drawn**: every unit, building and prop is a little model, a function from a point in space to a colour (`src/v2/sprites.ts`), which `src/v2/stack.ts` turns into a sprite by casting a ray per pixel. It lights each surface by which way it faces (so round things shade in bands), lets parts cast shadows on each other and the ground, darkens corners, and draws a lit rim and a dark line where one part stands in front of another. The art is drawn at twice the map's own grid (`ART` in `src/gfx.ts`): things are the same size on screen as before with pixels half as big, which is what leaves room for hatches, road wheels, window frames, roof clutter, courses of stone in the cliffs and blades of grass. The ground is painted to match (`paintSteps` in `src/v2/terrain.ts`), a few rows per frame so that painting a tile never holds up a frame. v1 is still drawn one for one.
